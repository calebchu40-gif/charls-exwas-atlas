# 2011 community interviewer observations: built environment and context

The 2011 Community Questionnaire's Part K instructs the interviewer to observe the sampled community and make judgments. Four additional candidate measures are now mapped separately: JK002 socioeconomic status (poor to rich), JK004 built-form organization (very disorganized to very organized), JK005 crowding (very crowded to very sparse), and JK006 accessibility for people with disabilities (no access to very convenient). Each is a seven-position ordinal scale with only the endpoints substantively anchored; codes 2–6 are retained as intermediate ordered ratings, not assigned invented category names.

These items are interviewer-observed community context. JK002 is not household income, JK005 is not population density, and JK006 is not an audited accessibility standard. They are single-wave, subjective measures and should not be interpreted as individual-level exposures without a justified community linkage and estimand. Higher JK005 values mean less perceived crowding; higher JK006 values mean better perceived accessibility. The scales are kept as four distinct concepts and should not be assumed to have equal intervals.

The questionnaire and Codebook extracts were checked for item identifiers and labels. The questionnaire supplies the bilingual endpoint anchors; the Codebook presents numeric response categories. No respondent records, Codebook frequencies, or frequency percentages were used. Source: `sources_official/CHARLS_2011_Community_Questionnaire_official_extracted.txt`, Part K, interviewer observations; `sources_official/CHARLS_2011_Codebook_extracted.txt`, corresponding JK002/JK004–JK006 entries.

The incremental inventory is generated by `scripts/build_community_exposure_crosswalk.py`; the anchor labels are maintained in `audit/community_questionnaire_label_overrides_v1.csv` and propagated by `scripts/build_community_value_labels.py`.
