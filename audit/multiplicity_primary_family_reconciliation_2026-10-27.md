# Multiplicity and primary-model reconciliation for the CHARLS atlas (2026-10-27)

## Why this review was needed

The current run plans define staged families (Wave 1 pilot K=105, the next household-environment block K=150, and the conditional heating block K=90) and adjust each block separately. Those blocks are useful for operational batching and QA, but separate within-block q values do not control the false-discovery rate over the eventual full CHARLS atlas. The reference NHANES paper reports significance over its complete computable atlas set: it gives 123,774 phenotype–exposure associations, Bonferroni alpha about 4 × 10^-7, and a 5% Benjamini–Yekutieli cutoff near 5.1 × 10^-4. It also reports survey-specific analyses for replication, while the main atlas estimate aggregates across independent NHANES survey samples. See the [Nature Medicine article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04266-0) and [NHANES implementation repository](https://github.com/chiragjp/nhanespewas).

CHARLS cannot copy that pooled NHANES estimand or its survey-cycle assumptions: CHARLS waves revisit many of the same people, use wave-specific samples/weights and measurement modules, and are not independent survey cycles. The project’s core analysis therefore remains wave-specific unless a separate repeated-measures estimand and method are justified.

## Binding interpretation for staged CHARLS batches

1. **One primary discovery model per declared hypothesis.** Before fitting the next authorized data run, freeze the primary model for each exposure–phenotype–wave hypothesis. Keep one model as the discovery model; retain M0 and M2 as descriptive/sensitivity specifications unless the protocol explicitly creates and labels a second co-primary family. Do not count multiple adjustment models as independent opportunities to declare the same pair a discovery.
2. **Batch corrections are provisional.** K=105, K=150 and K=90 block-level BY/Bonferroni outputs may be used to check code, inspect block-specific diagnostics, or report clearly labeled exploratory block findings after review. They must not be described as the final atlas-wide q values or used to decide which hypotheses enter later batches.
3. **Final atlas correction spans the full predeclared primary test set.** Once the candidate universe, release/wave scope, and primary model are frozen and all eligible batches have been run, construct one complete family containing every estimable primary exposure–phenotype–wave test in scope, including null results. Recalculate BY-FDR and Bonferroni across that family. Keep non-estimable hypotheses in the atlas with their reason/status; they do not receive fabricated p or q values. The family definition, number of estimable tests, exclusions, and code/data release IDs must be versioned.
4. **Conditional domains are not hidden.** The K=90 heating route is a different questionnaire-defined subdomain. Preserve its separate estimand and local block correction for interpretability, while also including its primary tests in the atlas-level superfamily if the public atlas makes atlas-wide discovery claims. Clearly state that its results do not estimate heating-fuel associations among everyone.
5. **Wave-specific dependence is respected.** Keep each CHARLS wave’s primary estimate wave-specific and include the in-scope wave-specific tests in the final atlas family. Do not treat the same participant’s repeated waves as independent replications or pool wave estimates using NHANES formulas. Any repeated-measures or cross-wave summary is a separate analysis with explicit within-person covariance handling, a distinct estimand, and its own method review.
6. **No outcome-driven expansion.** Null or unstable results do not authorize adding/removing exposures, phenotypes, categories, covariates, or waves based on observed p-values. A later block must be justified from source/measurement scope before its results are inspected.
7. **Pilot status is exploratory.** The original water/fuel sequence was extended after the initial water pilot, and some sensitivity choices were made after inspection of initial results. Multiplicity adjustment over K=105 does not erase that sequence. These outputs validate software behavior only; they are not confirmatory estimates and cannot be promoted as an independent confirmatory family.

## Adjustment-model alignment

The published NHANES paper's principal reported model is fully adjusted for age, age squared, sex, income, ethnicity, education, and survey year; it also evaluates eight alternative adjustment scenarios. This is not equivalent to labeling the CHARLS age/sex-only M1 as the NHANES-style primary model. The CHARLS pilot's M1 remains a minimally adjusted exploratory specification, while its M2 is a sensitivity specification selected for the pilot. Before the next authorized fit, the readiness reviewer must decide and freeze the CHARLS primary adjustment set for the intended estimand using a causal diagram and available, release-verified variables. Do not automatically add income/wealth, urban context, hukou, marital status, BMI, disease, or behaviors to every model: assess each variable as confounder, mediator, collider, or design/context variable for the particular exposure–outcome question. If a full model cannot be supported without major pair-specific missingness or invalid constructs, retain a clearly labeled common minimal model for comparability and state that its associations are less confounding-adjusted. Preserve alternative models as sensitivity results, not independent discoveries, unless a separate family is explicitly justified.

## Consequence for the current release gates

This reconciliation does not open any model gate. The four exact Wave 1 official files, source/frame reconciliation, final PSU/variance review, and the protected readiness record remain prerequisites. After those pass, the team may run the frozen blocks in sequence, but public significance fields must remain unset until the complete primary family and independent methods/disclosure reviews are complete. The reviewed-results exporter should receive a complete family with the final global adjustment, not only a selected block or significant rows.

## Source distinction

The Nature Medicine paper reports 123,774 computable associations and a global Bonferroni/BY summary. The GitHub snapshot’s `pipeline/pe_meta_adj.R` uses REML, while the separately defined UWLS function in `R/meta.R` was not called by that batch script in the reviewed commit; the paper/repository difference and the independent-cycle meta-analysis are documented in `audit/nhanespewas_github_method_review_v1.md`. For CHARLS we borrow the complete-family multiplicity principle and transparent model specifications, not the NHANES sample pooling.
