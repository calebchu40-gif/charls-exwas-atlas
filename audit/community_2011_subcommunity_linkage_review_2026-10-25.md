# 2011 community-to-person linkage boundary

The CHARLS 2011 National Baseline Users' Guide explains that the community questionnaire is answered by a village/neighborhood official. It also states that, for some very large neighborhoods, the sampled PSU could be a geographic subset of a broader neighborhood, and that 30 communities were split this way. The 2011 Codebook separately lists `communityID` and `sub commuID`; these identifiers must not be treated as interchangeable.

An aggregate-only review of the current local community and respondent preparation files found that `communityID` is not a unique row key in the community file, while the respondent preparation file does not contain `sub_commuID`. Candidate community fields differ across records that share a `communityID`. Because the local source archive identity has not been independently verified, the row-level QA counts and file hashes remain in the access-controlled local QA folder and are not copied here.

The repository now includes `scripts/audit_2011_community_subcommunity_linkage.py`. It reads only 2011 community candidate fields and the respondent `communityID` key, checks field presence and join-key structure, and writes its aggregate report to `%LOCALAPPDATA%\\CHARLS_ExWAS_Atlas\\qa_outputs`. It never merges the files, identifies communities, or emits source values. Synthetic tests cover duplicate keys, key coverage, missing keys, and the rule that a successful key-coverage check does not authorize a merge.

Until the authorized release documentation or a verified respondent-to-subcommunity crosswalk establishes which community record belongs to each respondent/household, do not join these candidates on `communityID` alone, deduplicate repeated rows, average subcommunity values, or publish person-level association results from them. The official community questionnaire is contextual metadata, not a personal exposure dose. Even after linkage is resolved, the target population, weights, PSU/variance design, and community-level clustering require separate analysis review.

Official source: [CHARLS 2011 National Baseline Users' Guide](https://charls.charlsdata.com/Public/ashelf/public/uploads/document/2011-charls-wave1/application/CHARLS_nationalbaseline_users_guide.pdf), sections 2.2.2, 3.1, and 4. This review uses no Codebook frequencies or respondent-level values.
