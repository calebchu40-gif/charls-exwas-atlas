# 2011 community-to-person linkage boundary

The CHARLS 2011 National Baseline Users' Guide says `communityID` is the usual key for linking community, household, and individual modules. It also explains that some very large neighborhoods were sampled as geographic sub-neighborhoods (30 communities were split this way), and that a sub-neighborhood PSU affects its selection probability. The 2011 Codebook separately lists `communityID` and `sub commuID`. The guide establishes the general linkage rule, but the current local file's repeated community keys require a release-specific reconciliation before applying that rule as a many-to-one merge.

An aggregate-only review of the current local community and respondent preparation files found that `communityID` is not a unique row key in the community file, while the respondent preparation file does not contain `sub_commuID`. Candidate community fields differ across records that share a `communityID`. Because the local source archive identity has not been independently verified, the row-level QA counts and file hashes remain in the access-controlled local QA folder and are not copied here.

The repository now includes `scripts/audit_2011_community_subcommunity_linkage.py`. It reads only 2011 community candidate fields and the respondent `communityID` key, checks field presence and join-key structure, and writes its aggregate report to `%LOCALAPPDATA%\\CHARLS_ExWAS_Atlas\\qa_outputs`. It never merges the files, identifies communities, or emits source values. Synthetic tests cover duplicate keys, key coverage, missing keys, and the rule that a successful key-coverage check does not authorize a merge.

The official guide supports `communityID` as the general link key. For the current local files, first verify release identity and determine whether the repeated rows represent the documented sub-neighborhood design or another source/version issue. Until that reconciliation or a verified respondent-to-subcommunity crosswalk establishes which record belongs to each respondent/household, do not treat `communityID` as a unique many-to-one key, deduplicate repeated rows, average subcommunity values, or publish person-level association results from them. The official community questionnaire is contextual metadata, not a personal exposure dose. Even after linkage is resolved, the target population, weights, PSU/variance design, and community-level clustering require separate analysis review.

Official source: [CHARLS 2011 National Baseline Users' Guide](https://charls.charlsdata.com/Public/ashelf/public/uploads/document/2011-charls-wave1/application/CHARLS_nationalbaseline_users_guide.pdf), sections 2.2.2, 3.1, and 4. This review uses no Codebook frequencies or respondent-level values.
