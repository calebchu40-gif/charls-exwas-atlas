# CHARLS wave sampling, weight, and biomarker scope audit (2026-10-22)

## What official documentation establishes

The CHARLS-hosted Harmonized CHARLS Version D codebook was additionally reviewed on 2026-10-24 as a secondary design source. It describes the sample as stratified by per-capita GDP for urban districts and rural counties, multistage county/district–village/community–household, and PPS; it identifies administrative villages/neighborhoods as PSUs. This adds documented context to the study's design description, but the codebook does not name a released variance-stratum field or specify an estimator. It therefore does not close the variance-design gate or authorize inferring strata from geography. See [`harmonized_charls_codebook_reference_review_2026-10-24.md`](harmonized_charls_codebook_reference_review_2026-10-24.md).

The official 2018 Wave 4 User Guide describes the baseline as a nationally representative sample of adults aged 45+ and spouses, drawn in 28 provinces, with 150 county/district units and 450 village/urban communities. The documented design is stratified and multistage PPS: county-level units were sorted by NBS region, urban/rural type, and per-capita GDP; then county/district, village/community, and household stages were sampled. Refreshment respondents were added in follow-up waves to maintain representation of younger age-eligible adults. By Wave 4, cross-sectional respondents plus spouses numbered 19,817. The guide explicitly says the cross-sectional sample is not necessarily the same as the panel sample.

For Wave 4 national cross-sectional inference, the User Guide recommends weights and describes four fields in `Weights.dta`:

- `HH_weight`: household base weight.
- `HH_weight_ad1`: household weight with household nonresponse adjustment.
- `INDV_weight`: individual base weight.
- `INDV_weight_ad2`: individual weight adjusted for household and individual nonresponse.

The weights apply to the main respondent and spouse, including proxy and partial interviews. Other interviewed people who are not eligible sample members receive no weight. The `cross_section` flag in `Sample_Info.dta` identifies the cross-sectional sample. The User Guide does not provide Wave 4 panel weights; panel weights require an analysis-specific attrition adjustment. These claims are transcribed from the official guide, not inferred from respondent records.

The guide says the PSU information is the same as previous waves. The official Wave 2 release note describes its `PSU.dta` as containing province, city name, and area type; it also says `communityID` remains the same between baseline and Wave 2 while household and individual identifiers changed and require documented adjustment. The 2011 baseline guide defines villages/neighborhoods as PSUs and describes the implicit stratification/sorting procedure. It also says that in some very large neighborhoods, selected geographic sub-neighborhoods served as PSUs and their selection probability was adjusted by their share of the parent village. The current available person/PSU metadata has `communityID` but no sub-neighborhood key; the community file separately carries `sub_commuID` for some records, with no respondent crosswalk found. This leaves two linked checks open: whether `communityID` alone is the correct first-stage variance PSU for all records, including split neighborhoods, and how a sub-neighborhood PSU is represented in the released design/weights. Do not infer or fabricate a sub-PSU or variance stratum.

These official sources do not identify an explicit released variance-stratum ID, replicate weights, or a complete design-based variance-estimation recipe. The existence of a stratified sampling design does not authorize constructing a stratum variable from geography or substituting area type for the actual design strata.

## Biomarker and age-domain implications

The 2018 guide says non-blood biomarkers (anthropometrics and blood pressure) were collected in Waves 1–3, while blood biomarkers were collected in Waves 1 and 3. Thus Wave 4 (2018) contributes no biomarker collection under this User Guide; the current catalog contains no Wave 4 fields in the biomarker category, consistent with the official schedule. It separately describes CHARLS-HCAP tests for respondents aged 60+ and their informants in Wave 4; the guide reports 11,021 respondents aged 60+ and their informants in HCAP, with a 99% response rate. HCAP is an age-restricted cognitive assessment, not a biomarker module. The guide does not document a dedicated HCAP nonresponse weight in the reviewed passage, so its analysis-specific weighting and domain handling remain to be checked against authorized released files and documentation.

The 2011 baseline User Guide and blood guide describe separate biomarker participation weights correcting for nonresponse; the 2013 Wave 2 Release Note explicitly releases a cross-sectional biomarker weight based on individual cross-sectional weights and a biomarker-response model, and says no longitudinal biomarker weight is provided. The 2015 official blood release note says the released blood file includes `Blood_weight` for the cross-sectional blood sample; panel blood weighting depends on the analysis. These weights must not be replaced by the general individual weight without an official basis. The 2020 User Guide also provides cross-sectional household/individual weights and says panel weights are analysis-specific. The public project bundle intentionally contains only guides/release notes; the owner's controlled derived tables do contain merged weight and sample-information fields, audited separately in [`prepared_wave_design_field_audit_2026-10-22.md`](prepared_wave_design_field_audit_2026-10-22.md).

## Project rules recorded from this review

1. Directory co-availability and Codebook frequency counts are not analytic sample sizes. Do not publish a pairwise N until authorized released data have been merged with the correct respondent/household IDs, cross-section/sample flags, wave/module eligibility, and special-code rules.
2. For person-level exposure–phenotype analyses, choose the weight to match the defined target population and unit; the four Wave 4 weights are not interchangeable. Do not automatically use household weights for person-level associations or apply individual weights to household-level estimands without defining the target.
3. For panel analyses, do not reuse Wave 4 cross-sectional weights as panel weights. Define the panel risk set and attrition/death treatment and construct/document an analysis-specific weight only after the official design and objective are fixed.
4. Do not claim complete design-based variance estimation until the released PSU and stratum identifiers and applicable variance method have been verified. Do not invent strata from region, province, urban/rural area, or community identifiers.
5. Do not treat 2018 HCAP as available to ages 45–59 or as a biomarker; do not assume a special HCAP weight without checking the released sample/weight files.

The current 2013 prepared table lacks the official `crosssection`/`longitudinal` indicators because the preparation routine dropped non-weight fields from `Weights.dta`; that routine is now fixed, but raw archives must be available to regenerate the private tables. Do not infer sample membership from weight missingness. Preserve file version IDs and checksums when rebuilding. For panel joins, follow official Wave 2 ID-adjustment instructions. CHARLS terms require academic use and prohibit publishing original microdata or granting others access; any public site may include only disclosure-reviewed, non-identifiable aggregate results. Until the 2013 flag is restored and the design variance method is verified, weighted pairwise sample sizes and design-adjusted association results remain uncomputed.
