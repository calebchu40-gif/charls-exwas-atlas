# 2011 CHARLS water-access × blood-biomarker pilot: plan and release gates

Date: 2026-10-26. This file records the pilot specification and execution state. It contains no participant-level records, pairwise sample counts, coefficients, or p-values. The actual private outputs stay under `%LOCALAPPDATA%\CHARLS_ExWAS_Atlas\pilot_2011_water_blood` and are not copied into the repository, GitHub, or Pages.

## Why this is a pilot

The public atlas remains a metadata catalogue; catalogue co-availability is not an analysis N. This first model run is a protected local workflow pilot intended to check that CHARLS fields, the blood-response weight, a candidate PSU, covariate handling, survey-engine output, and multiplicity correction can be connected end to end. It is not a formal, release-ready atlas result and is not displayed on the public site.

The pilot was chosen from the existing CHARLS field audits: Wave 1 has a baseline household running-water item (`i017`) and the separate blood-assay module, while the prepared Wave 1 table has a blood weight. Wave 3 blood assays are a separate official file that is not currently present locally; Wave 2 lacks its official sample flags in the prepared table. The 2011 pilot therefore avoids pooling waves or substituting a general interview weight for the blood-response weight.

## Frozen primary pilot specification

- **Wave and target:** 2011 baseline household-resident respondents aged 45 or older, with a valid `i017` response and positive `bloodweight`. This is a single-wave cross-sectional target, not a longitudinal estimate.
- **Age-domain specification:** the intended population is the household-resident age-45+ CHARLS baseline population. The official blood guide defines its blood target as main respondents and spouses, and says its blood-response correction was estimated using both groups. The baseline guide describes person-level inclusion probabilities for sampled eligible people and the additional main/spouse interview path. Therefore age 45+ should be treated as a survey domain inside the full blood-target design frame, not by deleting younger spouses before creating the survey design. The pilot code passes the complete prepared person table to `svy.Sample` and applies age/eligibility through `where=domain`, which is the correct domain-analysis structure in principle. This does not validate the age field, exact source weights, younger-spouse weight contribution, or national estimand; those still require exact-release/header and eligibility checks.
- **Exposure:** `i017`, “Does your residence have running water?”; code 1 = Yes and 2 = No. The contrast is Yes versus No. This is a household access proxy, not an individual water-quality or contaminant measurement.
- **Phenotypes:** the 15 Wave 1 blood analytes in the project crosswalk except cystatin C. Cystatin C is excluded because its age-stratified assay selection and corresponding selection-weight applicability have not been established. Collection/processing metadata are not treated as analytes.
- **M0:** exposure-only descriptive model.
- **M1 primary:** exposure + centered age + centered age squared + sex. Age is the existing provisional Harmonized CHARLS D-derived covariate; it is explicitly not represented as a raw CHARLS field or a fully re-derived official age value. The model uses an outcome-specific bloodweight-weighted SD scale and retains estimates on the reported assay unit scale.
- **M1 multiplicity:** the 15 exposure–phenotype hypotheses form a fixed family (`K=15`); report Benjamini–Yekutieli and Bonferroni adjustments. M0 is descriptive and is not a discovery family.
- **M2 sensitivity:** an additional model adds NBS urban/rural context and grouped 2011 education. It is a sensitivity analysis, not a second primary discovery family. The detailed grouping was set for this exploratory sensitivity after the initial M1 run and is not a preregistered confirmatory specification.
- **Missingness:** only the documented `i017` response codes 1/2 are valid. Structural nonresponse is not recoded as No. Negative assay values are treated as invalid; no distribution-based trimming or winsorization is used. Pair-specific complete cases are selected by the survey engine while retaining the full design frame.

## Survey design status and limits

- **Weight:** `bloodweight` is the Wave 1 blood-response weight candidate described in the CHARLS blood documentation. The current prepared table has a recorded SHA-256, but its original official archive identity/checksum is not present in the current local protected workspace. Weight provenance must be re-verified against the exact official source release before a result can be promoted.
- **PSU:** the protected local `psu.dta` has a unique `communityID` key, and every Wave 1 respondent community key maps to it. The pilot checks this mapping on every run and records the local PSU-map checksum. This verifies local key integrity, not the archive's release identity. The official baseline guide specifies village/neighborhood units as PSUs and says 30 very large communities were split into sub-neighborhood PSUs. Whether the released respondent `communityID` identifies the final sampled unit in all split cases remains unverified against the exact release/source mapping.
- **Variance:** the public baseline and blood guides describe sampling and response weights but do not specify a variance estimator, replicate weights, or an explicit variance-stratum field. The pilot uses a with-replacement, unstratified PSU Taylor approximation; this does not represent the described implicit sorting/stratification and is not a validated CHARLS design variance. `province`, `city`, `urban_nbs`, and `areatype` are not fabricated into a stratum; `urban_nbs` in M2 is only a contextual covariate.
- **Interpretation:** all estimates are exploratory, cross-sectional associations. A finite estimate, small p-value, or engine completion does not close the source/design gate, complete the pair-level estimability review, establish causality, or authorize public release.
- **Variance PSU caveat:** the official sample page states that 30 large communities were split into subareas when constructing the sample. The local respondent-to-`communityID` key match does not establish whether the released respondent key is the final variance PSU in those cases; treat this as unresolved design metadata, not merely a successful merge.

## Official age-domain and variance review (2026-10-26)

The official Wave 1 baseline guide describes a person-level weight built from the household weight and each age-eligible person's probability of selection, accounting for both the main-respondent and spouse interview paths. The Blood User Guide defines its target as all baseline main respondents and spouses, and constructs `bloodweight` from the individual weight with a blood-participation inverse-probability correction estimated on those same two groups (village dummies, age, sex, age-by-sex and education; inverse probability capped at the 99th percentile). For the project’s stated 45+ population, the analysis target should therefore be expressed as an age≥45 subpopulation of the full blood-target sample. The survey design must be created on the complete eligible frame before applying that domain. The current pilot code’s full-table `svy.Sample(...)` followed by `where=domain` follows this structure in principle. It still depends on confirming the official age source, relationship/eligibility fields, exact weight file, and that the candidate age field covers all relevant design-frame records.

The two public guides describe sample selection and nonresponse weights but do not state a variance estimator or provide variance-stratum/replicate-weight definitions. The baseline guide documents implicit sorting (region, urban/rural, GDP) and says 30 large communities were split into sub-neighborhood PSUs. This does not authorize us to invent design strata from geography or to assume the `communityID` map is the final PSU. Exact released PSU/Sample Info mapping or written CHARLS variance guidance, followed by method review, remains a promotion gate. Sources: [National Baseline User Guide, sampling and weights](https://charls.charlsdata.com/Public/ashelf/public/uploads/document/2011-charls-wave1/application/CHARLS_nationalbaseline_users_guide.pdf); [Blood Data User Guide, sampling and sample weights](https://charls.charlsdata.com/Public/ashelf/public/uploads/document/2011-charls-wave1/application/blood_user_guide_en_20140429.pdf).

## Execution state

`scripts/run_2011_water_blood_pilot.py` requires an explicit `--run` flag before it can read the private prepared Wave 1 table or private age candidate; `--help` and a no-argument invocation do not import analysis dependencies or access data. Use `python scripts/run_2011_water_blood_pilot.py --run` only after source/design gates are satisfied and an authorized analyst intentionally starts the exploratory refit. The household-environment extension has the same explicit-run guard. It removes participant IDs before constructing the analysis frame, uses the pinned `svy` engine, and writes all outputs only to the protected local directory. The run produced M0, M1, and M2 rows for the 15-analyte pilot; the M1 family returned finite model statistics for all predeclared pairs. A manual weighted least-squares/PSU-score calculation was compared with the survey engine for the M1 pairs. These facts validate the local software path only; they do not make any estimate release-ready.

The private result file includes raw and outcome-SD scales, model N, approximate design degrees of freedom, Kish effective N, model warnings, and multiplicity adjustments. The protected pair QA file contains pair-specific support diagnostics. Neither private output is tracked by Git.

The predeclared household-environment extension then fit 315 rows across M0/M1/M2, representing the fixed 105-coefficient family in each adjusted model; all 105 M1 and all 105 M2 terms returned finite statistics, with no survey-engine warnings. Fuel-category support was checked afterward as descriptive QA only (not a predeclared exclusion threshold); no hypotheses were removed based on it. Detailed estimates, p/q values, and pair-level support remain exclusively in the protected local output files. This extension is not confirmatory: the family selection followed inspection of the initial water pilot, and the approximation/design and confounding limitations remain.

## Predeclared exploratory extension: household cooking fuel

Before fitting the extension, the test family is set to the existing running-water contrast plus the six official 2011 cooking-fuel categories contrasted with electricity, across the same 15 non-cystatin-C blood outcomes: `K=105` coefficient hypotheses for M1 and separately `K=105` for the M2 sensitivity family. M0 remains descriptive. BY-FDR and Bonferroni use the full fixed family; unestimable coefficients remain in the family with null adjusted values. Fuel codes 1–7 are coal, natural gas, marsh gas, LPG, electric, crop residue/wood, and other; electricity (5) is the reference. No “clean/dirty” dichotomy is inferred, and blank responses are not converted to “no cooking.”

This extension is exploratory because it follows inspection of the first water pilot; correction over the 105 coefficients does not erase sequential screening. Its purpose is to exercise the end-to-end pair workflow across binary and multi-category household environment proxies. The official 2011 blood guide describes `bloodweight` as correcting household, individual, and blood-collection nonresponse by inverse predicted participation probabilities; its response model uses village dummies, age, sex, age-by-sex interactions, and schooling, with the inverse-probability factor capped at the 99th percentile. The weight addresses observed blood-response selection under a stated selection-on-observables assumption; it does not supply variance strata/replicate weights, authenticate the local file version, or make household proxies causal exposures. See the [official blood guide](https://charls.charlsdata.com/Public/ashelf/public/uploads/document/2011-charls-wave1/application/blood_user_guide_en_20140429.pdf) and [`housing_fuel_source_review_2026-10-24.md`](housing_fuel_source_review_2026-10-24.md).
