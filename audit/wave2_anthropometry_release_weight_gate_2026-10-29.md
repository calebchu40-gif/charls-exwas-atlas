# Wave 2 anthropometry release and biomarker-weight gate review

**Date:** 2026-10-29  
**Status:** official documentation and the protected official All Data bundle/header support the Wave 2 module and a cross-sectional weight candidate; pair eligibility, age domain and variance remain open. This memo does not authorize association fitting.

## Evidence reviewed

- CHARLS Wave 2 Release Note, VersionID 20151118: its released-module table identifies `Biomarker.dta` as anthropometric measurements. The note separately says `Biomarker_weight` is cross-sectional and adjusts for household/individual nonresponse through an inverse predicted probability of responding in the Biomarker module, capped at the 99th percentile. It explicitly offers no longitudinal biomarker weight.
- Official Wave 2 Biomarker Questionnaire and Codebook document these anthropometric measurement fields: `qi002` standing height (cm), `qj002` arm length (cm), `qk002` knee height (cm), `ql002` body weight (kg), and `qm002` waist circumference (cm). The questionnaire defines 993 as tried but unable to measure and 999 as chose not to measure; both are nonmeasurement statuses. Preserve measurement context separately: `qi003` compliance; `qj003` arm side and `qj004` compliance; `qk003` leg side and `qk004` compliance; `ql003` floor surface, `ql004` footwear and `ql005` compliance; `qm003` difficulties and `qm005` bulky clothing. The body-weight route item PL001 marks eligibility only below 150 kg. The standing-height section's QI001 question stem says “breathing test”; do not use that inconsistent stem alone to define its denominator. These instrument/codebook details do not establish participant-level eligibility or final numeric-validity rules.
- The official Wave 2 All Data package (release item 321, 2015-11-18) was found in the protected private raw intake and matched the official release checksum recorded in the verified intake manifest. A protected Wave 2-specific intake copy was created. Metadata-only archive inspection found the expected `Biomarker.dta`, `Weights.dta`, `PSU.dta` and questionnaire modules. Metadata-only Stata-header review confirmed the five documented anthropometry fields in `Biomarker.dta`, the respondent keys, and the `crosssection`, `longitudinal` and `Biomarker_weight` fields in the bundled `Weights.dta`. This verifies archive provenance and headers only; it does not establish pair coverage, value validity, sample eligibility or variance design. No participant values, IDs or estimates were read.
- The earlier prepared person-level table remains unsuitable as source evidence by itself because its own manifest does not bind it to an authenticated archive. It must be rebuilt from the now-verified release bundle (or independently reconciled to it) before use.

## Analysis implication

For a **Wave 2 cross-sectional** candidate pair where a source field is confirmed to come from `Biomarker.dta`, `Biomarker_weight` is the source-grounded starting weight because it accounts for response to that module. The analysis frame must also apply the official `crosssection` flag, confirm the field's actual release/module placement and valid route, and define pair-specific eligibility and missingness. This weight only documents the Biomarker response adjustment; it does not automatically correct nonresponse in a separate exposure module or establish complete pair coverage. Do not substitute `INDV_weight_ad2` without a target-specific rationale.

Do not use `Biomarker_weight` for a longitudinal/within-person estimand: the Wave 2 release explicitly provides no longitudinal biomarker weight. Baseline-to-Wave-2 identifier transformation is needed only for actual baseline linkage/panel estimands; a wave-specific cross-sectional analysis should use the Wave 2 identifiers and sample definition.

## Protected Wave 2 frame rebuild and age/target review

- The checksum-verified official bundle was processed on the protected server with `prepare_charls_wave_tables.py` for Wave 2 only. The person-level output, module merge QA, variable-collision map and source-bound manifest remain in protected storage. The rebuilt respondent spine has unique person IDs; Biomarker, weight flags/weights, and the five anthropometry fields are present. No cross-wave merge or association model was run. Exact counts and matching rates remain in the private QA output, not this public memo.
- The CHARLS official sample description defines the target as people aged 45+ living in households and their spouses; the Wave 4 user guide also states that Wave 2 added the Wave 1 43–44-year-old refreshment respondents and their partners. Therefore do **not** apply a blanket current-age-45+ exclusion to the Wave 2 `crosssection` sample: that would remove age-eligible partners and could misstate the official cross-sectional target. Use the official `crosssection` membership flag as the starting sample definition, then define an age domain only for a clearly specified estimand.
- Wave 2 `ba004` is not a universal age field. The official Wave 2 questionnaire routes directly from confirmation of the preloaded birth date to `ba005` when the stored date is correct; it asks `ba004` only when the birth date is unknown/refused. Thus `ba004` is structurally unavailable for most of the released person frame and cannot serve as a general age adjustment or eligibility filter. For age adjustment, reconstruct age from verified birth date and interview time, using the official solar/lunar calendar indicator and conversion procedure; for linked baseline respondents, follow the Release Note's prescribed Wave 1-to-Wave 2 ID transformation. Validate the resulting age logic and missingness before including it in a model. Do not fill missing ages with an assumed threshold or use `ba004` alone.
- A private aggregate-only frame QA confirmed the rebuilt table's ID integrity, sample-flag and weight coverage, and per-measure nonmissing/special-code profiles. These counts are restricted to the protected QA record. They do not authorize pair selection, prove that the biomarker-weight construction applies to every non-Biomarker exposure module, or resolve the variance design.
