DATA ALERT
The CHARLS Team
February 4, 2023
The CHARLS 2018 wave included a module, CHARLS-HCAP, intended to assess the cognitive sta-
tus of our respondents 60 years and older. It has come to our attention that one of our assessment
instruments, the Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE), which is a widely used cognition assess-
ment, is likely biased because the Chinese version of MMSE was validated only among people who
can read and write and those without vision impairments and in mandarin.
In particular, the following questions require literacy:
• dc019_w4: Read the words on the screen, then do what it says.
• dc023_w4: Write any complete sentence
• dc024_w4: Copy the overlapping pentagons
The following questions require vision:
• dc019_w4: Read the words on the screen, then do what it says.
• dc016_w4: Naming a watch
• dc017_w4: Naming a pencil
• dc024_w4: Copy the overlapping pentagons
In addition, word lists used for memory tests are paraphrased in mandarin and may disadvantage
non-mandarin speakers:
• dc013_w4_1 and dc015_w4: Word list recalls
• dc018_w4: Repeating a phrase
Additionally, some other items in the CHARLS-HCAP may be biased as well. One example is an
HRS-TICS item, “dc026_w4. What do you call the kind of prickly plant that grows in the desert?”
Because the cactus is a less-known plant in China, many people failed to answer correctly, especially
less educated people. Another example is CERAD 10-word recall. Some words on the list, such as
butter and queen, are not familiar to many Chinese, especially among less educated people.
We are working on detecting biases of MMSE and other instruments against the illiterate, vision
impaired and non-mandarin speakers and are finding ways to correct the biases. Once this is finished,
we will update the data. Meanwhile, users are advised to use the data with caution.
